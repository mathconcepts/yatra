## Problem Statement

Your request, verbatim: "This needs to be a Geographical exploratory map route map with animation. Check if Google maps or related can be used. Reimagine what can be done possible for my usecase. similar to https://politypolicy.com/VandeBharat but for places with satellite images."

Yatra today is a one-journey-at-a-time viewer. You pick Tirupati or Konkan, press play, and one marker crawls one route over Esri imagery and 3D terrain. The reference site is the opposite shape: one dark map of India, 168 train dots, a live IST clock that puts every dot where its timetable says it should be right now, a "Move the clock" scrubber with day tabs and "Play the day", a "Moving now" list with progress percentages, and a Sources & Method essay that is half the product's credibility.

The gap is not prettier flyovers. It is breadth plus time: many things alive at once on one photographic Earth, with the clock (and the calendar) as the primary control, and a way to dive from that overview into the 3D journey Yatra already renders.

## What Makes This Cool

**The map moves too.** The VandeBharat site moves dots across a static map. Yatra can move the ground under them. NASA GIBS serves MODIS and VIIRS imagery as a free, key-less WMTS with a `TIME` dimension, and EOX publishes yearly Sentinel-2 cloudless mosaics. So the calendar scrubber does two things at once: the 10111 Konkan Kanya creeps down the coast by timetable while the Western Ghats turn from May brown to August monsoon green beneath it. Flip the Night toggle and scrub to Diwali, and the VIIRS night-lights layer flares over Tirupati. Scrub to January and the Char Dham snowline sits where the trek is closed. Nobody has built "where is everything, right now, on the real Earth, in this season" for India.

The honest caveat, up front: daily true-colour imagery over the Konkan from June to August is mostly cloud. The brown-to-green scrub therefore runs on cloud-free composites (16-day vegetation index or monthly Blue Marble), not on daily scenes; daily true colour is a "today" view only. Details in the imagery ladder.

**Globe to doorstep in one camera move.** MapLibre 6 ships globe projection with atmosphere (the repo is on 4.7). The entry shot is Earth from space on Sentinel-2 cloudless, a continuous flyTo lands on India with every journey glowing, a click on a pulsing dot dives through zoom 5 to zoom 13 into the existing Atlas with Esri imagery and terrain. That dive is also the Director's new opening shot: every reel starts from orbit.

**Scheduled, not tracked, and proud of it.** The reference's honesty ("A train running ninety minutes late is still drawn on time here") is a feature. Yatra already has the per-journey `blurb` and `culture` scaffolding; a Sources & Method panel per journey is the same grammar.

**Beyond your own task.** The same surface, pointed elsewhere: a Brahmotsavam processions timeline for a temple town (where is the chariot at 17:00 on day 7); a school geography lesson ("find today's monsoon front"); a rail-fan's "every train on the Konkan line right now" once a second timetable is typed in; a Sabarimala season view where the November to January pilgrim surge renders as particle flow along the footpath rather than one marker. I would try the monsoon scrub over Konkan first. You already own the route, the camera engine and the postcards.

On-map attribution strings, set on each MapLibre source's `attribution` field and stamped into exported reels and postcards by the existing Director attribution line: `Sentinel-2 cloudless © EOX IT Services GmbH (CC-BY 4.0), contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data`, `Imagery courtesy NASA GIBS / ESDIS`, `Terrain © Mapzen / Amazon AWS`, and `Rail geometry © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) via OpenRailwayMap`. The same lines go in `docs/ATTRIBUTION.md`.

**Flag (see something, say something):** Yatra fetches Esri World Imagery from the key-less `server.arcgisonline.com` endpoint. An Esri staff reply on the Esri Community (Dec 2024) says that service "is not free, it can only be used with an ArcGIS Online or ArcGIS Enterprise license" and "is not available for commercial use". The clean fix is an ArcGIS Location Platform API key: 2 million basemap tiles per month free, then $0.15 per 1,000, same imagery, one URL change plus a `token` param. Mapbox (50k free loads/month, satellite included) and MapTiler (100k free requests, non-commercial) are the fallbacks if you would rather not create an Esri account. `REPO_MODE` is unknown so this is a flag, not a fix. **No-key behaviour decided in this design:** slice 2 removes the key-less Esri URL from the default build; a build without `VITE_ESRI_API_KEY` serves the Journey band from EOX Sentinel-2 (10 m) so "key-free by default" stays true at reduced resolution, and the Esri key is the opt-in that restores sub-metre imagery. **This fallback is app-wide, not Living Atlas only:** `basemapStyles.js` is shared, so in a key-free build the existing Atlas, Reels and Director surfaces, and every exported reel and postcard, drop from sub-metre Esri imagery to 10 m Sentinel-2 close in. That is a visible change to current behaviour and is part of the call in Open Question 4. Open Question 4 can still swap Esri for Mapbox or MapTiler; the fallback rule does not change.

## Constraints

- Google content cannot share a screen with MapLibre content (ToS above).

## Premises

1. **The "whoa" is many things alive at once on one photographic Earth, with time as the control.** Not a single prettier flyover. Evidence: your word was "places", plural, and you named a 168-dot map as the model.
2. **Two clocks, not one.** Clock time (IST, scrub the day) drives scheduled movers: trains, darshan slots, tour windows. Calendar time (scrub the year) drives the Earth: GIBS composite imagery for seasons (daily true colour only as a Today view) and the daily VIIRS band behind a Night toggle. Both share one scrubber UI with a mode toggle. *(Revised after the second opinion; see below.)*
3. **Google Maps is the wrong base for Yatra** (mixing ban, no video export, India 3D coverage unconfirmed). Keep MapLibre; the Director MP4 export is worth more than photoreal cities you probably cannot get for India anyway.
4. **Build on LocationConfig; add, do not replace.** Exactly three optional schema additions, all on routes: `schedule` (slice 1: one timetabled run with halts, or a walk window), `geometry` (slice 1: `"surveyed" | "approximate"`, consumed by the Sources line and the engine tests; **absent means `approximate`**, so an un-annotated route is labelled "drawn straight between halts" until someone surveys it), and `services[]` (slice 4: several timetabled runs on one route). No new fields on landmarks. The registry in `src/config/index.js` already is the "roster".
5. **Satellite at India scale comes from Sentinel-2 cloudless; journey scale is Esri on a Location Platform key, falling back to Sentinel-2 when no key is set.**
6. **Do-nothing cost is real but quiet.** Nothing breaks. Yatra stays a viewer, and the thing you pointed at (one map, many movers, live clock, provenance essay) never exists.
7. **Distribution is solved.** Same Pages deploy, same Worker, no new infra.

## Recommended Approach

**B in four slices.** Each slice is independently shippable and keeps the test suite green.

1. **Slice 1, "Where The Journeys Are" (= Approach A).**
   - **Schema** (`src/config/schema.js`), one optional block per route:
     ```js
     // Timetabled run (one train, one tour)
     schedule: {
       kind: "timetable",
       tz: "Asia/Kolkata",
       days: ["Mon","Tue","Wed","Thu","Fri","Sat","Sun"],
       halts: [                       // ordered; bound to route geometry by landmark id
         { landmark: "mumbai-cst",  dep: "07:05" },
         { landmark: "ratnagiri",   arr: "11:50", dep: "11:55" },
         { landmark: "mangaluru",   arr: "21:35" },
       ],
       source: { name: "ixigo timetable, compiled 2026-10", url: "https://..." },
     }
     // Walk window (pilgrim path)
     schedule: {
       kind: "window",
       tz: "Asia/Kolkata",
       open: "04:00", close: "22:00",
       paceKmh: 3,                    // elevation-aware: slowed by local slope from profileStats
       departuresPerDay: 7,           // the "sample pilgrim count"; markers depart evenly spaced
       source: { name: "TTD footpath hours", url: "https://..." },
     }
     ```
     Halts bind to the route by landmark id; `scripts/snap-landmarks.mjs` asserts each named landmark is within 300 m of the route line and records its distance along the route. Konkan's existing station landmarks (Panvel, Roha, Ratnagiri, and so on) become halts; stations without a landmark get one. **Where the scripts run:** both `scripts/import-rail-geometry.mjs` (`npm run import:rail`) and `scripts/snap-landmarks.mjs` (`npm run snap:landmarks`) run on demand on the developer's machine and their outputs are committed; the Cloudflare Pages build has no network dependency. The snap assertion also runs inside vitest against the committed data, so a halt that drifts off its route fails `npm test`, not the deploy.
   - **Engine** (`src/services/schedule.js`, pure): `moversAt(route, schedule, dateTimeIST)` returns an array of movers `{ id, lon, lat, elev, progress, state }`. A timetable yields one mover; a window yields `departuresPerDay` movers, departures evenly spaced between `open` and the latest start that still arrives by `close`, each with its own progress. `state` is one of `before | moving | held | arrived | rest-day`; `held` is a train standing at a halt between `arr` and `dep`. The cumulative-time table is built once per route from the halts (distance along route per halt, linear interpolation by distance between halts); `interpolateRoute` and `distanceKm` from `src/utils/route.js` do the geometry. **`progress`** is the distance along the route from the first halt to the mover, divided by the distance from the first halt to the last halt, clamped 0 to 1; for a window the first and last "halts" are the route's endpoints. If a timetable's halts do not span the whole geometry (a service that terminates short of the line's end), the untravelled stretch is drawn dimmed, the mover never enters it, and `progress` still reaches 1 at the last halt. **Zero-departure windows:** `departuresPerDay: 0`, or a window too short for even one walk to finish by `close` (the latest valid start precedes `open`), both yield zero movers and a plain dot; the test exercises both, and `snap-landmarks` warns at the second case. Outside a window every mover is `before` or `arrived` and the journey shows as a plain dot. "Moving now" counts movers in `moving` or `held`.
   - **Track geometry** (the open TODO, done here): `scripts/import-rail-geometry.mjs` pulls the Konkan line from OpenRailwayMap/OSM via Overpass, simplifies to about 2,000 points, writes `src/config/geometry/konkan-railway.json`, and adds the ODbL attribution line to `docs/ATTRIBUTION.md`. **How the file reaches the route:** the script emits an array of Place objects (`{ lat, lon, elev }`, with `elev` linearly interpolated along the line between the nearest existing station waypoints, no DEM fetch), and the Konkan config imports the JSON and uses it as the route's `waypoints`. No further schema field. Routes carry `geometry: "surveyed" | "approximate"` (absent means `approximate`); an approximate route still animates (the engine does not care) and its Sources line says "drawn straight between halts", exactly as the reference does.
   - **Surface** (`src/components/living/LivingAtlas.jsx`): map at India extent, one GeoJSON source for all route lines, one for all movers updated per rAF tick, left column (title and counts from the registry, IST clock, day tabs, scrubber, play 1m/5m/20m, Moving-now list), and a one-line Sources footer per journey (`schedule.source` link plus "scheduled, not tracked").
   - **Clock state across surfaces:** the Living Atlas writes its clock to the URL as `?surface=living-atlas&t=2026-10-09T20:32&speed=5m&mode=day` (debounced, `history.replaceState`). There is no `day` parameter: the day tab is derived from the weekday of `t`, and picking a tab rewrites `t` to the same time on that weekday. Clicking a dot navigates to `?surface=atlas&location=<id>&from=living-atlas&t=<t>&speed=<speed>&mode=<mode>`; the Atlas shows a "Back to the atlas" link that returns all three, so the round trip is lossless. With no `t` present the Living Atlas starts on the live IST clock at 5m speed in day mode. No store, no localStorage.
   - **Tests:** `schedule.test.js` covers before departure, after arrival, rest day, midnight wrap, held at a halt, `departuresPerDay: 0`, a window too short for one walk, a window with seven departures, a window queried outside its hours, a timetable whose last halt is short of the geometry's end, and an approximate-geometry route; `snap-landmarks` asserts every halt resolves.
2. **Slice 2, imagery ladder.** `basemapStyles.js` grows `eox-s2` and `gibs` sources with a zoom-banded `raster-opacity`: Sentinel-2 below zoom 10, Esri above when `VITE_ESRI_API_KEY` is set, Sentinel-2 at all zooms when it is not; the key-less Esri URL is removed from the default build. The tile chain learns the new sources. Spike, before any UI: fetch GIBS tiles over the Konkan for 1 June, 15 July and 30 August for the 16-day vegetation composite, the monthly Blue Marble set and the daily true-colour layer; the vegetation composite is the Season layer unless the spike finds it unusable over the Konkan, in which case Blue Marble monthly takes its place; record the confirmed layer identifiers in this doc. Wireframe note: the "Season" panel is off by default on mobile; the GIBS layer is visible to zoom 7, fades 7 to 8, off above 8.
3. **Slice 3, globe and dive.** `maplibre-gl` to 6.x; `projection: globe` at the World band; a `dive(config)` helper in `moodCamera.js` that emits a `CameraStep[]` from zoom 1.5 to the journey's `topography.zoom`; the Living Atlas entry shot is World to Region; a Director scene type `orbit-dive` uses the same plan. The perf probe gates globe on mobile the way it gates terrain today.
4. **Slice 4, calendar clock and many movers.** Scrubber gains a `mode: "day" | "year"` toggle; year mode binds `TIME=` on the single Season layer chosen in slice 2 and snaps to its cadence (16-day, or monthly on the Blue Marble fallback). Two further toggles, Night (VIIRS day/night band, daily cadence) and Today (daily true colour, current date only), swap the layer; the user never picks a GIBS layer by name. Routes may carry `services[]` (several timetabled runs sharing one geometry, each with its own halts and days); `moversAt` returns one mover per service. Rendering stays per-marker GeoJSON until the perf probe records a median frame time above 41 ms on the Pixel 6a reference; only then is deck.gl `TripsLayer` (interleaved with MapLibre) adopted, which is the same trigger named under Approach B and Dependencies. Full Sources & Method panel per journey, generated from `schedule.source`, `services[].source`, `geometry`, and the imagery attribution strings.

Wireframe of the Region level (rough, intentionally unstyled): `docs/designs/yatra-living-atlas-wireframe.html` in the repo (self-contained, open it in a browser; a screenshot was also sent to you in the session). Its Season panel caption says "NASA GIBS daily imagery"; that caption predates the composite decision above and the panel should read "NASA GIBS seasonal composite" when built. It shows the three-band crumb (World · Region · Journey), the left column in the reference's grammar (title and counts from the registry, IST clock, Move the clock, Play the day, Season scrubber, Moving now), real-geometry route lines with pulsing dots only for moving things, a globe pip to zoom out, and the Sources & Method panel.
